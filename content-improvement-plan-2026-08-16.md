## 3. Priority work

### P0 — Fix the two largest problems first

#### A. Expand `/service-areas/kansas-city-mo`

This page accounts for one-third of all findings and currently lacks the richer `priorityContent` used on stronger city pages.

Add:

1. **A specific opening:** Explain the kinds of decisions Kansas City, MO homeowners face across older neighborhoods, tight lots, elevated yards, Northland properties, and Kansas City weather. Avoid claims about permits, codes, or neighborhoods unless verified.
2. **A three- or four-part service focus:**
   - Custom deck design and new builds
   - Deck repair and condition assessment
   - Deck replacement and layout improvement
   - Stairs and railings, if a fourth item fits the template
3. **A repair-versus-replacement section:** Cover visible condition, repeated repairs, framing, stairs, railings, current footprint, access, maintenance goals, and long-term use. Link to both dedicated service pages and the repair-or-replace guide.
4. **Planning priorities:** Furniture and circulation, stair direction, grade and yard access, shade, material maintenance, and planning a future cover or screen room.
5. **Project proof:** Two relevant project cards with real scope, materials, challenge, and result. Clearly say when a project is representative of Kansas City metro work rather than located at a claimed Kansas City address.
6. **Four useful FAQs:** Service availability, repair versus replacement, wood versus composite, and the quote/assessment process.
7. **Contextual links:** Link to custom decks, repair, replacement, stairs and railings, the repair-or-replace guide, and two project case studies.

Recommended content target: roughly 800–1,100 useful words after template text, with no paragraph copied from another city page.

#### B. Correct `/about` intent without keyword stuffing

Do not rewrite About to target “cedar deck builder Kansas City,” “deck builder Leawood,” or “deck builders Raymore.” Those are service/location searches.

Turn About into the strongest entity-and-trust page on the site:

1. Add a concise company story: who DecksRXKC is, why the business focuses on deck work, and the principles behind its approach.
2. Add verified facts only: owner/team names, years of experience, insurance or licensing language, warranties, brands/materials used, service radius, and review totals. If a fact is not confirmed, leave it out rather than using a vague claim.
3. Add original team/work-process photography and captions.
4. Expand “How We Work” into concrete steps from inquiry and site review through options, proposal, build communication, and closeout.
5. Add a short “What we build and where” section that links to the correct service and city pages, especially custom decks, Leawood, Raymore, and Kansas City, MO.
6. Add two or three attributed customer-proof excerpts, keeping the visible source context accurate.
7. Use an About-focused title/H1 and structured data; do not insert city-service keyword lists into headings.

At the same time, strengthen `/service-areas/raymore-mo`, confirm the existing Leawood page is internally linked, and make a deliberate decision about cedar. The success condition is that the correct landing page becomes the most relevant URL—not that About earns a higher score for the wrong query.

## 4. Page-writing standard

Every revised local/service page should pass this checklist:

- One primary intent and one clearly identified primary URL.
- Unique title, meta description, H1, intro, service focus, planning guidance, project proof, and FAQs.
- The city/service phrase appears naturally in the title, H1, opening, one relevant subheading, and internal-link context—not in every paragraph.
- At least three decision-helpful details a homeowner can act on.
- At least one verified proof element: real project, attributed review, original photo, or confirmed business fact.
- Relevant internal links in both directions between city, service, guide, and project pages.
- Clear next-step CTA after the main decision content and at the end.
- Visible FAQ answers match any FAQ structured data exactly.
- No invented local project, permit, code, warranty, certification, brand, schedule, or pricing claim.
- No near-duplicate city copy with only place names swapped.

## 7. Measurement and acceptance criteria

### Before publication

- All nine flagged URLs have an assigned primary intent.
- All five generic service-area gaps—Kansas City, MO; Kansas City, KS; Liberty; Blue Springs; and Raymore—have unique priority content.
- About contains verified trust content and is not optimized as a substitute for city/service pages.
- Every revised page has at least two relevant contextual internal links and receives at least one reciprocal link.
- No unsupported cedar, local-project, permit, code, warranty, or credential claim is published.

### After publication

Track at 14 days, 30 days, and 60 days:

1. **Correct URL ownership:** In Google Search Console, the intended page—not About or Projects—should receive impressions for each commercial query family.
2. **Content score movement:** Use the same tool/settings as the source workbook. Aim first to move scores below 55 by at least 10 points and move the flagged set out of the tool's poor-quality band; treat this as a diagnostic, not the business KPI.
3. **Organic behavior:** Impressions, clicks, non-brand entrances, quote-form starts, click-to-call events, and conversion rate by landing page.
4. **Internal discovery:** Confirm important links are crawlable and the revised pages remain indexed with the intended canonical.
5. **Evidence gap:** Maintain a backlog for pages that still lack verified project proof or original imagery.

If the correct page gains impressions but the score remains weak, improve missing decision content and proof. If About continues to rank for city/service terms, strengthen the intended landing page and internal-link anchors instead of adding more transactional copy to About.
